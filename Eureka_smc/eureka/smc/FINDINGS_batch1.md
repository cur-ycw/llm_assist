## The core tradeoff (Cartpole-scale, N ≤ 8)

You cannot get both strong selection and lineage diversity at small N:

- **β_target = 2** → healthy: N=8 keeps ≥6 lineages, reaches λ=1 in 3 stages, for any κ.
  But β=2 is weak terminal selection (π₁ ∝ p₀·exp(2R) is a flat tilt).
- **β_target ≥ 5** → strong selection but full collapse to 1 lineage at N≤8.
- To run **β_target = 5** without collapse you must raise **N ≥ 16** (→ 2× the RL cost).

This is consistent with review finding #3 (RL seed noise dominates acceptance at
β≈5): β=2 also makes acceptance `exp(β·ΔR)` far less noise-driven.

## Recommended Batch-2 defaults

- **Cartpole bring-up:** `N=8, β_target=2, κ=0.5` (`max_delta=1/3`, `max_iters=15`).
  Healthy, cheapest, validates the scaffold end-to-end.
- **If stronger selection is wanted later:** `N=16, β_target=5, κ∈{0.5,0.9}` (still ≥4
  lineages). Do NOT ship the plan's config default `N=8, β=5, κ=0.9` — it collapses.

## Caveats

- The collapse metric depends on `jitter` (models MH proposals diverging) and the fixed
  synthetic regimes. The *qualitative* result (β≥5 collapses at small N) is robust across
  regimes; the exact β/N boundary is a model estimate, not a guarantee. Re-run with
  `--jitter` / `--beta` sweeps once the live evaluator gives real score-spread statistics.
- κ mostly trades stage-count for min-ESS here; it is a weaker lever than β and N for
  collapse at small N.
